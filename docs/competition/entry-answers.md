# Entry form answers, Tata Varsity Pitch 2026

Category: Digital, Health & Deeptech.
Deadline: 23:59 BST, 18 October 2026. Eligibility: founder-led, under
GBP 125,000 funding received. Every claim below is either demonstrated in
the demo, cited from published research, or flagged as still to verify.

## What: the idea and the problem

Seamly reconciles the systems a mid-market company already runs: the ERP
that holds orders and invoices, the haulier records that hold deliveries,
the spreadsheets that hold project and service work. It finds where the
records disagree, prices every disagreement in pounds from the contracted
price book, routes it to a named owner, and tracks the money recovered.
The problem is real and quantified: UK companies estimate 5.87 percent of
revenue goes uncollected each year, around 244 billion pounds, and seven in
ten say they cannot stop it. Most of it is not fraud; it is records that
never meet between systems.

## Why: what is different

Three things. First, pounds, not error lists: every exception carries a
stored formula over named records and the contract price book, so finance
can rank by impact and audit any figure to the transaction. Second, the
mid-market wedge: enterprise process mining takes months and six figures;
Seamly onboards from spreadsheets and CSV exports in days, which is where
62 percent of UK firms still manage revenue. Third, the loop: exceptions
get owners, recoveries get recorded, and the board shows money recovered,
not just found. Where an AI analyst exists, it only narrates what the
engine computed, with citations that a deterministic verifier checks; an
unverified answer is discarded, never shown.

## Who: customers and evidence

The buyer is the CFO or financial controller of a UK mid-market company
(roughly 20 to 250 million pounds turnover): distributors, light
manufacturers, food producers. The daily users are finance and ops teams
who currently reconcile in Excel. Published evidence: 73 percent of finance
teams admit they cannot quantify their leakage; mid-market studies tie
billing-error leakage to late deliveries, chargebacks and unbilled work.
The founder has hands-on experience building QC and traceability software
for a chilled-food manufacturer, including batch economics and retailer
chargeback behaviour. Still to verify (in progress): 10 to 15 structured
interviews to confirm which leak is biggest and who will pay.

## Where: market and competitors

UK mid-market first. Above it, Celonis serves enterprises with deployments
measured in months; Duco reconciles banks, not order books; below it,
everyone uses spreadsheets. The mid-market gap is: too complex for
spreadsheets, too small for enterprise process mining. Food manufacturing
is the flagship vertical: 43 percent of food and drink businesses reported
cost increases above 10 percent, and food operations produce exactly the
event records that reconcile badly with finance. Adjacent rings:
distributors and light manufacturers, then the same engine for services
firms. The published market numbers are directional vendor research; the
discovery interviews exist to test them against real companies.

## How: the business model

Annual SaaS subscription per site, tiered by revenue band and number of
connected sources, positioned against recovered money: the demo dataset
shows 15,110 pounds at risk on roughly 1.4 million pounds of synthetic
revenue, about 1 percent, at the low end of the published 1 to 5 percent
range. A payer-back of 10x on subscription is the sales frame. CSV-first
onboarding makes a two-week paid pilot practical; connectors (Sage, Xero,
NetSuite) are the year-two expansion, priced as add-ons. The recovery
ledger is the retention mechanism: cancelling means losing the proof of
what is still leaking.

## When: plan, team, resources, risks

Now: a working product slice, not a slide: ingestion, seven reconciliation
rules, pound scoring, the resolution loop, the digest, and a
citation-verified analyst, tested end to end with a golden dataset. Next
three months: discovery interviews, then a pilot on one company's real
exports. 2027: connectors and the food vertical rule pack. Team: the
founder builds; a finance-domain advisor is the first hire of the prize
money. Risks, honestly: data quality (mitigated by explainable formulas
and drill-down), trust in AI output (mitigated by the verifier: the engine
computes, the model narrates), integration maintenance (deferred by
CSV-first), and competitors moving down-market (the wedge is speed and
price, defended by the recovery loop as switching cost).
