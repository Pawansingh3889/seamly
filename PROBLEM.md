# Seamly , Problem Statement

> **Last updated:** 28 August 2026
> **Update rule:** this file changes whenever discovery evidence arrives.
> It is the living record of the bottleneck hypothesis and what supports or
> breaks it.

## The hypothesis

UK mid-market companies (roughly 20 to 250M pounds turnover) run finance in
one system and operations in others: the ERP holds orders and invoices,
hauliers hold delivery records, project and service work lives in
spreadsheets. Nobody reconciles them continuously, so money leaks at the
seams: goods delivered but never invoiced, contracted rates not applied,
duplicates paid, service work completed but unbilled.

The claim to test: **mid-market UK companies lose 1 to 5 percent of revenue
to these gaps, cannot quantify the loss, and would pay for a system that
prices it, routes it, and proves recovery.**

## Published evidence (directional, all vendor research, to be verified)

- Sagacity, Missing Billions: UK companies estimate 5.87 percent of revenue
  goes uncollected, about 244 billion pounds a year; 7 in 10 know leakage is
  a problem but do not know how to prevent it; nearly half traces to manual
  data entry across systems and poor reconciliation. Surveyed telco, energy,
  insurance and water.
- Clari research: 62 percent of UK firms still collect, manage and analyse
  revenue in Excel. Validates CSV-first onboarding.
- 2026 CFO analyses: 73 percent of finance teams admit they cannot quantify
  their leakage. Awareness is universal; quantification is the gap. This is
  the gap Seamly's pricing engine addresses.
- Mid-market vulnerability: leaner finance teams, OTIF tracked in
  spreadsheets and carrier portals, supplier delays turning into chargebacks,
  refunds and unbilled work. Studies of service-heavy businesses put billing
  error leakage near 10 percent of revenue.
- Sector pressure making CFOs receptive: food inflation forecast above 9
  percent for 2026, 43 percent of food and drink businesses reporting cost
  increases over 10 percent, EU export friction. Margin pressure drives the
  hunt for recoverable revenue.

## What we must verify in discovery (the claims that could break the build)

1. Who feels the pain: CFO, financial controller, or ops manager. Who pays?
2. What systems the mid-market actually runs (expect Sage, Xero, NetSuite,
   D365, spreadsheets) and what exports they can produce.
3. Which leak is biggest in practice: delivered-not-invoiced, rate errors,
   duplicates, unbilled service, chargebacks. Rule pack order follows this.
4. What a mid-market buyer pays today to not solve this (manual
   reconciliation hours, finance headcount).
5. Whether the 1 to 5 percent figure survives contact with real mid-market
   companies or only holds in large enterprises.

## Positioning decisions

- Generic engine, specific first wedge: UK mid-market, food manufacturing as
  the flagship proof point (domain depth from prior work), distributors and
  light manufacturers as the wider ring.
- Does not replace systems. Sits above them. CSV/XLSX first, API connectors
  later.
- The differentiator is not detection, it is pounds: every exception priced
  from the contract price book, ranked, owned, and traced to recovery.
- Competition context: Tata Varsity Pitch 2026, Digital/Health/Deeptech
  category, entry deadline 18 October 2026.
